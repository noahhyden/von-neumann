\section{Conclusion}

Adding a finite speed of information to the self-replicating slingshot model costs redundant
travel, not fill time. Once the dynamics are resolved so that each probe decides at its true
arrival time, the tens-of-percent fill-time penalty seen in a fixed-timestep model collapses: it
was a discretization artifact. The slingshot speed-up survives intact, reproducing Nicholson and
Forgan to the same order of magnitude.

What the delay costs instead is wasted travel. Every star is still settled, with a negligible
change in the number of probes built, but stale views send probes to stars others have already
claimed. The delay multiplies the wasted journeys by at most $1 + v/c$, a bound we both derive and
measure, so the tax scales with the probe's speed in units of light, $\Lambda = v/c$. Its
coefficient stays below one and shrinks with system size: about $0.8$ on a few-thousand-star field,
where the tax runs from negligible at the powered cruise through about a percent at slingshot
speeds to a sixth at directed-energy speeds, but only about $0.07$ on our quarter-million-star
headline field, where even the directed-energy fuel tax is about $1.3\%$. The baseline is itself
wasteful, about nine in ten journeys even with a perfect map, so this is a $v/c$ surcharge on an
already-saturated process.

The law survives the variations we tested. It holds under a clumpy field, where if anything it
softens, and it grows with the replication branching factor without saturating up to sixteen
offspring. Whether it also costs energy is mission-dependent: only a rendezvous that must brake
pays for a wasted trip's kinetic energy, which for the fast policies was largely gravitationally
sourced rather than fuelled. Scale decides which cost dominates. On the small fields of earlier
work the tax is mostly fuel and effort, with a smaller fill-time penalty alongside it; at galactic
scale the two invert, the fuel tax saturating toward a percent while the fill-time lag, set by the
front crossing an ever-larger field, grows to exceed it.

The tax is not a hard floor. A probe that listens in flight, one of the two extensions the model
invites, recovers essentially all of the wasted-arrival tax at a small cost in fill time, while a
full probe-to-probe relay of the settled map remains open. How much either buys back is itself
bounded by the finite speed of light.
